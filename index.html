04

General Requirements

4.1 Impartiality  ·  4.2 Confidentiality

Clause 4.1 · Impartiality

Impartiality

4.1.1 Laboratory activities shall be undertaken impartially.
Examples
  • An impartiality policy signed by top management, displayed in the laboratory and communicated to all staff.
  • Annual signed declarations from every employee and contractor confirming no conflicts of interest.
  • An impartiality statement included in customer contracts and test/calibration reports.
4.1.2 Top management shall demonstrate commitment to impartiality.
Examples
  • Management review minutes showing impartiality is a standing agenda item with decisions recorded.
  • Top management signs the impartiality policy and participates in impartiality risk reviews.
  • Resources allocated for impartiality training, awareness sessions and conflict-of-interest reviews.
4.1.3 The laboratory shall be responsible for impartiality and shall not allow commercial, financial or other pressures to compromise it.
Examples
  • The laboratory refuses a testing job where the customer demands a predetermined result.
  • Separation of commercial and technical functions with different reporting lines to prevent sales pressure on test outcomes.
  • A documented procedure to declare and manage situations where staff have financial interests in customer organisations.
Clause 4.1 · Impartiality (continued)

Impartiality risks & mitigation

4.1.4 The laboratory shall identify risks to its impartiality on an ongoing basis — including risks from its activities, its relationships, and the relationships of its personnel.
Examples
  • An annual impartiality risk assessment covering activities, commercial relationships and personnel relationships.
  • A risk register listing threats such as a sister company that supplies products to the same customers the laboratory tests.
  • Ongoing monitoring — new risks identified whenever a new customer, method or staff member joins.
4.1.5 The laboratory shall eliminate or minimize the risks to impartiality identified.
Examples
  • Rotation of staff to avoid long-term relationships with specific customers.
  • Removal of staff from testing where a personal relationship exists with the customer or their competitor.
  • Documented mitigation actions in the risk register with responsible persons and review dates.
Clause 4.2 · Confidentiality

Confidentiality

4.2.1 The laboratory shall be responsible for the confidentiality of information obtained or created during laboratory activities, and for the proprietary rights of its customers.
Examples
  • Signed confidentiality agreements with employees, contractors and temporary staff.
  • Written confidentiality clauses in every customer contract.
  • Secure storage — locked cabinets and password-protected electronic files for customer information.
4.2.2 The laboratory shall notify its customer when it is legally obliged to release confidential information.
Examples
  • A contract clause stating the laboratory will notify the customer before legally compelled to disclose information.
  • A procedure requiring management approval and customer notification before releasing information to authorities.
  • Records of any disclosure with the date, authority, reason and customer notification.
4.2.3 Information about the customer received from sources other than the customer shall be kept confidential; the source shall not be revealed to the customer unless agreed by the source.
Examples
  • Separate handling of third-party information — for example a supplier's complaint about a customer's product.
  • A procedure stating the source is not revealed unless the source agrees.
  • Records of consent obtained before sharing source information with the customer.
4.2.4 Personnel shall keep confidential all information obtained or created during the performance of laboratory activities.
Examples
  • Confidentiality clauses in employment contracts and contractor agreements.
  • Annual refresher training on confidentiality obligations with attendance records.
  • Access controls — passwords, locked rooms and clean-desk policy — limiting information to authorised personnel only.
05

Structural Requirements

5.1 – 5.7  ·  Legal entity, management, scope, organisation & responsibility

Clause 5.1 – 5.3

Legal entity, management & scope

5.1 The laboratory or the organisation of which it is part shall be a legal entity legally responsible for its laboratory activities.
Examples
  • Business registration certificate (e.g. SSM certificate for companies in Malaysia) kept on file.
  • For government laboratories — the establishing Act or statutory instrument that creates the legal entity.
  • The legal entity status documented in the quality manual with the registration number.
5.2 The laboratory shall identify management that has overall responsibility for the laboratory.
Examples
  • An organisation chart showing the laboratory's position and reporting lines to top management.
  • A job description for the laboratory manager defining overall responsibility and authority.
  • An appointment letter or delegation of authority for the laboratory manager.
5.3 The laboratory shall define and document the range of laboratory activities for which it conforms to MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — covering permanent facilities, sites away, temporary/mobile facilities and customer facilities — conforming to customer and regulatory requirements.
Examples
  • A scope of accreditation document listing each test/calibration method and the location where it is performed.
  • A quality manual section describing permanent, mobile and customer-site activities.
  • Regulatory approvals or licences (e.g. from DOSH, MOH, AELB) required for specific activities, kept on file.
Clause 5.4 – 5.5

Organisation & responsibility

5.4 Laboratory activities shall be performed in a way that meets the requirements of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017, the laboratory's customers, regulatory authorities and the organisations providing recognition.
Examples
  • Documented procedures for each test/calibration method, aligned to the standard and customer requirements.
  • Contract review records showing customer specifications have been checked and can be met.
  • Records of compliance with regulatory requirements — for example environmental permits or safety approvals.
5.5 The laboratory shall: a) define the organisation and management structure; b) specify responsibility, authority and interrelationship of all personnel; c) document necessary procedures to ensure consistent application of activities and validity of results.
Examples
  • a) An organisation chart with management, technical and support functions clearly separated.
  • b) Job descriptions for all key personnel, including deputies and approved signatories.
  • c) Documented standard operating procedures for each activity, reviewed and approved.
Clause 5.6 – 5.7

Personnel & communication

5.6 The laboratory shall have authorized and sufficient personnel to: a) implement, maintain and improve the management system; b) identify deviations and initiate actions; c) report to management on performance and improvements; d) ensure effectiveness of laboratory activities.
Examples
  • a) A management system implementation team with defined roles and responsibilities.
  • b) A deviation reporting procedure with trained personnel authorised to initiate actions.
  • c) Management review meeting minutes with performance reports and improvement recommendations.
  • d) Internal audit team and competency assessment records showing effectiveness is monitored.
5.7 The laboratory shall communicate to staff about the effectiveness and integrity of the management system when changes are made to it.
Examples
  • Staff briefing sessions when the quality manual or key procedures are revised.
  • Email or notice-board announcements of management system changes with acknowledgement records.
  • Training records confirming staff have been informed and understand the changes.
06

Resource Requirements

6.1 General  ·  6.2 Personnel  ·  6.3 Facilities  ·  6.4 Equipment  ·  6.5 Traceability  ·  6.6 External providers

Clause 6.1 – 6.2 · Personnel

Resources & competence requirements

6.1 The laboratory shall have sufficient personnel, facilities, equipment, systems and support services necessary to manage and perform its laboratory activities.
Examples
  • A staffing plan showing adequate personnel for current and forecast workload.
  • A maintenance schedule for facilities and equipment with budget allocation.
  • IT support and LIMS contracts in place to support laboratory activities.
6.2.1 All personnel acting in the laboratory shall be impartial, competent and work in accordance with the management system.
Examples
  • Signed impartiality declarations and competency assessments for every staff member.
  • Training records showing staff know and follow the management system.
  • Performance evaluations linked to management system compliance.
6.2.2 The laboratory shall document the competence requirements for each function that may influence results — including education, qualification, training, technical knowledge, skills and experience.
Examples
  • A competency matrix listing required education, training, skills and experience for each function.
  • Job descriptions specifying the competence requirements for each role.
  • Training needs analysis derived from the documented competence requirements.
Clause 6.2 · Personnel (continued)

Competence, communication & authorisation

6.2.3 Personnel shall have the competence to perform the laboratory activities for which they are responsible.
Examples
  • Competency assessment records for each analyst — witnessed tests, proficiency samples, blind duplicates.
  • Qualification certificates — degrees, diplomas, professional certifications.
  • Records of successful proficiency testing participation by individual analysts.
6.2.4 The duties, responsibilities and authorities of laboratory personnel shall be communicated to them.
Examples
  • Signed job descriptions acknowledging the duties, responsibilities and authorities.
  • An organisation chart showing interrelationships and reporting lines.
  • Authorisation letters for approved signatories and specific test methods.
6.2.5 The laboratory shall have procedures and retain records for: a) determining competence requirements; b)–f) selection, training, supervision, authorization and monitoring of competence.
Examples
  • a) A procedure for determining competence requirements for each function.
  • b)–f) Selection, training, supervision, authorisation and monitoring procedures.
  • Training records, supervision logs, authorisation lists and competency review records.
6.2.6 Personnel shall be authorized to perform specific laboratory activities.
Examples
  • An approved signatory list for each test/calibration method.
  • Authorisation records for sampling, testing, calibration, reporting and giving opinions.
  • Records of withdrawal or suspension of authorisation when competence lapses.
Clause 6.3 · Facilities

Facilities & environmental conditions

6.3.1 The laboratory shall document and ensure control of the facilities and environmental conditions necessary for laboratory activities.
Examples
  • Procedures for controlling access, temperature, humidity, lighting and cleanliness.
  • Facility layout drawings showing controlled areas and their boundaries.
  • Records of facility control measures — HVAC maintenance logs, pest control records.
6.3.2 Facilities and environmental conditions shall be suitable and shall not adversely affect the validity of results.
Examples
  • Environmental monitoring data showing conditions are within specified limits.
  • Separation of incompatible activities — sample preparation away from calibration benches.
  • Corrective action records when conditions exceed limits, with impact assessment.
6.3.3 The laboratory shall monitor, control and record the environmental conditions.
Examples
  • Temperature and humidity logs (manual or automated) for calibration rooms.
  • Records of monitoring frequency and alert limits with responsible personnel.
  • Maintenance records for monitoring equipment — thermometers, hygrometers, data loggers.
Clause 6.3 · Facilities (continued)

Facility control & off-site activities

6.3.4 The laboratory shall monitor and periodically review the measures to control facilities — including access, prevention of contamination, and separation of incompatible activities.
Examples
  • Quarterly review of access control logs and facility inspection reports.
  • Annual review of contamination prevention measures — cleanroom protocols, clothing change procedures.
  • Corrective action records from facility reviews with follow-up verification.
6.3.5 The laboratory shall ensure compliance to Section 6.3 when laboratory activities are performed at sites or facilities outside its permanent control.
Examples
  • Field test procedures with environmental checks performed at the site before testing.
  • Records of temporary facility setup and controls at customer sites.
  • Mobile laboratory procedures with environmental monitoring records during off-site work.
Clause 6.4 · Equipment

Equipment — availability, handling & verification

6.4.1 The laboratory shall have access to equipment required for the correct performance of laboratory activities, and which can influence results.
Examples
  • An equipment master list matching equipment to each test/calibration method.
  • Records showing equipment is available when needed — utilisation logs, booking systems.
  • Replacement or backup arrangements for critical equipment to avoid disruption.
6.4.2 The laboratory shall ensure compliance to Section 6.4 when using equipment outside its permanent control.
Examples
  • Field equipment check procedures performed before use at customer sites.
  • Transport and storage procedures to protect equipment during off-site work.
  • Records of equipment checks before and after off-site work.
6.4.3 The laboratory shall have a procedure for handling, transport, storage, use and planned maintenance of equipment.
Examples
  • Equipment handling and transport procedures with packaging and shock protection requirements.
  • Storage conditions specified for sensitive instruments — temperature, humidity, dust.
  • Planned maintenance schedule with completed maintenance records.
6.4.4 The laboratory shall verify that equipment conforms to specified requirements before being placed or returned into service.
Examples
  • Installation qualification records for new equipment before first use.
  • Verification records after repair or recalibration before returning to service.
  • Check records before returning equipment to service after a fault or modification.
Clause 6.4 · Equipment (continued)

Calibration & identification

6.4.5 / 6.4.6 Measuring equipment shall be calibrated and capable of achieving the measurement accuracy or measurement uncertainty required to provide a valid result.
Examples
  • Calibration certificates showing the required uncertainty is achieved for the intended use.
  • Measurement uncertainty budgets demonstrating fitness for purpose.
  • Records of calibration interval justification based on stability, usage and manufacturer recommendations.
6.4.7 The laboratory shall have an established calibration programme and shall review the suitability and frequency of calibration.
Examples
  • Documented calibration programme with intervals, due dates and responsible personnel.
  • Annual review of calibration intervals based on stability data and usage patterns.
  • Records of interval adjustments with technical justification.
6.4.8 Equipment requiring calibration or which has a defined period of validity shall be labelled, coded or identified.
Examples
  • Calibration labels on all equipment with the calibration due date.
  • Colour-coded tags showing calibration status — current, due, out of service.
  • Equipment ID numbers linked to the equipment master list and calibration records.
Clause 6.4 · Equipment (continued)

Malfunction, checks & adjustments

6.4.9 The laboratory shall have a process for handling equipment that no longer meets specified requirements, including examining the effect of the deviation.
Examples
  • Out-of-service tag procedure with defined responsibilities and authorities.
  • Impact assessment records showing which results may be affected by the nonconforming equipment.
  • Customer notification records when nonconforming equipment affected reported results.
6.4.11 The laboratory shall perform periodic checks or updates to maintain confidence in equipment performance — including reference values or correction factors.
Examples
  • Daily balance checks with records and acceptance criteria.
  • Weekly thermometer verification against a reference thermometer.
  • Interim checks between annual calibrations with defined acceptance criteria and corrective actions.
6.4.12 The laboratory shall take practicable measures to prevent unintended adjustments of equipment from invalidating results.
Examples
  • Seals or tamper-evident labels on calibration adjustments.
  • Password protection on digital instrument settings to prevent unauthorised changes.
  • Training and procedures prohibiting unauthorised adjustments, with consequences for breach.
Clause 6.4.13 · Equipment Records

Equipment records

6.4.13 The laboratory shall retain records for equipment which can influence laboratory activities — including a) identity with software/firmware version; b) manufacturer, model, serial number; c) verification evidence; d) current location; e) calibration dates, results, adjustments, acceptance criteria, due dates; f) reference material documentation; g) maintenance plan and records; h) damage, malfunction, modification, repair details.
Examples
  • a) Equipment ID with software/firmware version recorded in the equipment register.
  • b) Manufacturer, model and serial number recorded for each item.
  • c) Verification records showing conformance to specified requirements.
  • d) Current location recorded and updated in the equipment register.
  • e) Calibration dates, results, adjustments, acceptance criteria and due dates on file.
  • f) Reference material documentation with results and validity periods.
  • g) Maintenance plan and completed maintenance records.
  • h) Damage, malfunction, modification and repair records with dates and actions.
Clause 6.5 · Metrological Traceability

Metrological traceability

6.5.1 The laboratory shall establish and maintain metrological traceability by means of a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the measurement uncertainty.
Examples
  • Calibration certificates showing the chain from working standard to reference standard.
  • Traceability charts for each measurement parameter, showing each link and its uncertainty contribution.
  • Uncertainty statements for each link in the chain, with the combined uncertainty at the working level.
6.5.2 Measurement results shall be traceable to the International System of Units (SI) where possible.
Examples
  • Calibration certificates from a national metrology institute (NMI) with SI traceability statements.
  • Reference standards calibrated by an accredited laboratory with documented SI traceability.
  • Records of SI realisation for base units where the laboratory performs primary calibration.
6.5.3 Where SI traceability is not possible, traceability shall be to an appropriate reference — such as calibration by a competent laboratory, certified reference materials by a competent producer, or direct realisation of SI units.
Examples
  • Certified reference materials (CRMs) with traceability statements and uncertainty values.
  • Consensus standards used for material testing where SI traceability is not practical.
  • Documented justification for using non-SI traceable references, approved by the technical manager.
Clause 6.6 · Externally Provided Products & Services

Externally provided products and services

6.6.1 The laboratory shall ensure the suitability of externally provided products and services that affect laboratory activities.
Examples
  • An approved supplier list with evaluation criteria and performance monitoring notes.
  • Purchase specifications defining technical requirements for calibration services, reference materials or subcontracted tests.
  • Records of incoming inspection or verification of purchased items before use.
6.6.2 The laboratory shall have procedures and records for: a) defining, reviewing and approving requirements for external products/services; b) criteria for evaluation, selection, monitoring and re-evaluation of external providers.
Examples
  • a) A procedure for defining, reviewing and approving requirements for external products and services.
  • b) A procedure for evaluation, selection, monitoring and re-evaluation of external providers.
  • Supplier evaluation records and performance monitoring reports with re-evaluation dates.
6.6.3 The laboratory shall communicate its requirements to external providers for the products and services to be provided.
Examples
  • Purchase orders specifying technical requirements, traceability and uncertainty.
  • Contracts with external calibration providers stating the required traceability and reporting requirements.
  • Records of communication with external providers about changes to requirements or specifications.
07

Process Requirements

7.1 – 7.11  ·  The technical heart of the standard

Clause 7.1 · Review of Requests, Tenders & Contracts

Contract review & customer notification

7.1.1 The laboratory shall have a procedure for the review of requests, tenders and contracts — ensuring requirements are defined, capability and resources exist, and the appropriate method is selected.
Examples
  • A documented contract review procedure with defined responsibilities.
  • Completed contract review forms for each customer request.
  • Records showing capability, resources and method selection were checked before acceptance.
7.1.2 The laboratory shall inform the customer of inappropriate or outdated methods; ensure deviations do not impact integrity or validity; ensure the decision rule is defined and agreed when a statement of conformity is required; and notify customers of contract deviations.
Examples
  • Records of customer notification when a requested method is inappropriate or outdated.
  • Documented decision rule (e.g. simple acceptance, guard band) agreed with the customer for conformity statements.
  • Records of repeated contract review and customer notification when deviations occur after work has commenced.
Clause 7.1 · Contract Review (continued)

Cooperation & method selection

7.1.3 The laboratory shall cooperate with customers or their representatives in clarifying the request and in monitoring the laboratory's performance.
Examples
  • Records of customer visits or teleconferences to clarify requirements.
  • Customer feedback forms and action records from performance monitoring.
  • Witnessing records when customers observe testing or calibration activities.
7.1.4 Methods shall be selected and verified or validated in accordance with Clause 7.2.
Examples
  • Method selection records referencing Clause 7.2 requirements.
  • Verification or validation records for the selected methods before use.
  • Customer agreement records confirming the selected method is acceptable.
Clause 7.2.1 · Selection & Verification of Methods

Method availability & selection

7.2.1.1 All methods, procedures and supporting documentation shall be appropriate, relevant, up to date, and readily available to personnel.
Examples
  • A controlled method library with current versions available at the bench.
  • Records of method review and update showing documents are current.
  • Access logs showing personnel can readily access the methods they need.
7.2.1.2 The laboratory shall ensure that it uses the most appropriate version of a method.
Examples
  • Document control records showing the current version is in use at the bench.
  • Records of obsolete methods withdrawn from use and archived.
  • Version comparison records when methods are updated, with impact assessment.
7.2.1.3 The laboratory shall select and inform the customer of the method chosen if the customer does not specify the method.
Examples
  • Customer communication records informing them of the method chosen.
  • Contract review records documenting the method selection and customer agreement.
  • Records of customer acceptance of the selected method before work commences.
Clause 7.2.1 · Methods (continued)

Verification, development & deviations

7.2.1.4 The laboratory shall verify that it can properly perform the methods and achieve the required performance before introducing them.
Examples
  • Verification records showing the laboratory can perform the method.
  • Performance data demonstrating required performance is achieved.
  • Records of method verification before first use, signed by the technical manager.
7.2.1.5 Records of verification shall be retained.
Examples
  • Verification reports with results and acceptance criteria.
  • Records of the verification procedure used for each method.
  • Sign-off by the technical manager confirming verification is complete.
7.2.1.6 The laboratory shall have a formal plan for method development, performed by competent personnel with adequate resources, with periodic reviews to ensure customer needs are fulfilled.
Examples
  • Documented method development plan with resources, timelines and responsibilities.
  • Records of competent personnel assigned to development activities.
  • Periodic review records showing customer needs are fulfilled by the developed methods.
7.2.1.7 Any deviations to methods shall be documented, justified, authorized and accepted by the customer prior to performing them.
Examples
  • Documented justification for each method deviation, with technical rationale.
  • Customer acceptance records obtained before performing the deviated method.
  • Authorisation records from the technical manager approving the deviation.
Clause 7.2.2 · Validation of Methods

Validation of methods

7.2.2.1 The laboratory shall validate non-standard methods, laboratory-developed methods, standard methods used outside their intended scope, and validated methods that have been modified.
Examples
  • Validation reports for laboratory-developed methods with full performance data.
  • Records of validation when a standard method is used outside its scope.
  • Re-validation records when a validated method is modified.
7.2.2.2 Validation records shall include the procedure used, specification of requirements, performance characteristics, results obtained, and a statement on the validity of the method.
Examples
  • Validation procedure documents describing the approach used.
  • Performance characteristics data — selectivity, linearity, accuracy, precision, uncertainty.
  • Statement of fitness for intended use signed by the technical manager.
7.2.2.3 The performance characteristics of validated methods shall be relevant to the customer's needs and consistent with specified requirements.
Examples
  • Comparison of method performance against customer specifications.
  • Records showing method performance meets regulatory requirements.
  • Customer agreement records confirming the method is fit for the intended purpose.
Clause 7.3 · Sampling

Sampling

7.3.1 The laboratory shall have a sampling plan and method — available at the sampling site — addressing the factors to be controlled to ensure the validity of subsequent results.
Examples
  • Documented sampling plan available at the sampling site.
  • Sampling method describing the factors to be controlled — temperature, containers, preservation.
  • Records of sampling plan review and update with justification.
7.3.2 Sampling data shall be recorded as part of the laboratory activity.
Examples
  • Sampling records with date, time, location and sampler identity.
  • Environmental conditions during sampling recorded and linked to the sample.
  • Sample identification and transportation records showing chain of custody.
7.3.3 Deviations from the sampling plan or method shall be recorded and reported.
Examples
  • Records of deviations from the sampling plan or method, with justification.
  • Customer notification records for sampling deviations that may affect results.
  • Impact assessment records for deviations, with corrective action where needed.
Clause 7.4 · Handling of Test or Calibration Items

Handling of test or calibration items

7.4.1 The laboratory shall have a system for unambiguous identification of items that ensures no confusion, with the identification retained whilst under the laboratory's responsibility.
Examples
  • Unique sample ID labels that prevent mix-ups between samples.
  • Records showing identification is retained throughout custody.
  • Barcode or LIMS-based sample tracking for unambiguous identification.
7.4.2 The laboratory shall take precautions to avoid deterioration, contamination, loss or damage to items during handling, transport, storage, preparation and retention.
Examples
  • Handling and storage procedures for samples with special requirements.
  • Records of sample condition checks at receipt and before testing.
  • Environmental control records for sample storage areas.
7.4.3 Upon receipt of items that have deviated from specified conditions, the laboratory shall record the deviations and notify the customer for instructions, recording the consultation results.
Examples
  • Records of deviations from specified conditions at receipt.
  • Customer notification records with the instructions received.
  • Records of the consultation outcome and any impact on the work.
7.4.4 For items requiring storage under specific environmental conditions, the laboratory shall maintain, monitor and record these conditions.
Examples
  • Temperature and humidity logs for sample storage areas.
  • Monitoring records with alert limits and corrective actions when exceeded.
  • Maintenance records for storage equipment — refrigerators, freezers, environmental chambers.
Clause 7.5 · Technical Records

Technical records

7.5.1 The laboratory shall retain and maintain technical records with sufficient information to enable repetition under conditions as close as possible to the original — including date, personnel identity, factors affecting results, and uncertainty evaluation.
Examples
  • Records with date, personnel identity, raw data and calculations.
  • Factors affecting results and uncertainty recorded alongside the results.
  • Records sufficient for another analyst to repeat the activity under similar conditions.
7.5.2 Amendments to technical reports shall be trackable to previous versions or original observations; original and amended data and files shall be kept — including date, altered aspects, and personnel responsible.
Examples
  • Amendment log with date, altered aspects and responsible person.
  • Original and amended data kept together for traceability.
  • Audit trail in the LIMS showing changes and who made them.
Clause 7.6 · Evaluation of Measurement Uncertainty

Measurement uncertainty

7.6.1 The laboratory shall identify the contributions to measurement uncertainty using appropriate methods, including those arising from sampling.
Examples
  • Uncertainty budget identifying each contribution and its magnitude.
  • Records including sampling contributions where applicable.
  • Documented method for uncertainty analysis — GUM, Monte Carlo, etc.
7.6.2 For laboratories performing calibrations, including of its own equipment, the measurement uncertainty for all calibrations shall be evaluated.
Examples
  • Uncertainty budgets for each calibration method and range.
  • Records showing uncertainty is evaluated for all calibrations, including internal ones.
  • Comparison of uncertainty against customer requirements and method specifications.
7.6.3 For laboratories performing testing, the measurement uncertainty shall be evaluated. If not possible, an estimation shall be made.
Examples
  • Uncertainty budgets for each test method where feasible.
  • Estimation records where full evaluation is not possible, with justification.
  • Records showing uncertainty is considered in conformity statements.
Clause 7.7 · Ensuring the Validity of Results

Ensuring the validity of results

7.7.1 The laboratory shall have a procedure for monitoring the validity of results, planned and reviewed periodically.
Examples
  • Documented quality control procedure with planned monitoring activities.
  • Monitoring plan with frequency and acceptance criteria.
  • Periodic review records of the monitoring plan and its effectiveness.
7.7.2 The laboratory shall compare its results with other laboratories — for example through proficiency testing or inter-laboratory comparisons.
Examples
  • Proficiency testing participation records and results with satisfactory outcomes.
  • Inter-laboratory comparison records for methods where PT is not available.
  • Investigation records for unsatisfactory results, with corrective actions.
7.7.3 The laboratory shall analyse monitoring data and take appropriate action when results fall outside pre-defined criteria.
Examples
  • Control charts with pre-defined criteria for triggering action.
  • Investigation records when results fall outside criteria.
  • Corrective action records linked to monitoring results and their effectiveness reviewed.
Clause 7.8.1 – 7.8.2 · Reporting of Results

Reporting — review, authorisation & common content

7.8.1.1 The laboratory shall review and authorize the results prior to release. Any results not reported to the customer shall be made readily available.
Examples
  • Report review checklist completed before release.
  • Authorisation signatures on reports before issue.
  • Records of unreported results being made available on request.
7.8.2.1 The report shall contain at least: a) title; b) laboratory name/address; c) location of performance; d) unique identification; e) customer name/contact; f) method identification; g) item description/condition; h) receipt and sampling dates; i) performance dates; j) issue date; k) sampling plan reference; l) results relate only to items; m) results with units; n) deviations/exclusions; o) authorising person; p) external provider identification.
Examples
  • Report template containing all required elements a) through p).
  • Review checklist confirming all elements are present before release.
  • Records showing each report includes title, lab address, unique ID, method, item, dates, results, authorisation.
7.8.2.2 When applicable, the laboratory shall clearly identify in its reports which information and/or sample is provided by the customer.
Examples
  • Reports clearly marking customer-supplied data or samples.
  • Records of customer agreement to use their information.
  • Documentation showing the source of each item or data point in the report.
Clause 7.8.3 · Specific Requirements for Test Reports

Test reports

7.8.3 The test report shall contain, where necessary: a) specific test conditions (e.g. environmental); b) statement of conformity where relevant; c) measurement uncertainty when relevant or required; d) opinions and interpretations where appropriate; e) additional information required by methods, authorities or customers.
Examples
  • Test reports stating environmental conditions — temperature, humidity during testing.
  • Measurement uncertainty stated when it affects conformity to a specification limit.
  • Opinions and interpretations included with the basis documented and authorised personnel identified.
Clause 7.8.4 · Specific Requirements for Calibration Reports

Calibration reports

7.8.4 The calibration report shall contain: a) measurement uncertainty in the same unit as the measurand; conditions under which calibrations were made; a statement of metrological traceability; results before and after adjustment or repair; statement of conformity where relevant; opinions and interpretations where appropriate.
Examples
  • Calibration certificates with uncertainty stated in the same unit as the measurand.
  • Metrological traceability statement identifying how the measurements are traceable.
  • Results before and after adjustment or repair included when available.
7.8.4.3 Calibration certificates or labels shall not contain any recommendation on the calibration interval unless agreed with the customer.
Examples
  • Calibration certificates without interval recommendations as standard practice.
  • Records of customer agreement when an interval recommendation is included.
  • Labels that do not state a calibration interval unless agreed with the customer.
Clause 7.8.5 · Specific Requirements for Sampling Reports

Sampling reports

7.8.5 The sampling report shall contain: date of sampling; unique identification of the item/material; location of sampling including diagrams/sketches/photographs; reference to sampling plan and method; environmental conditions during sampling; information needed to evaluate measurement uncertainty.
Examples
  • Sampling reports with date, location, sampling plan reference and method.
  • Environmental conditions during sampling recorded and reported.
  • Information for evaluating measurement uncertainty included for subsequent testing or calibration.
Clause 7.8.6 – 7.8.7

Statements of conformity & opinions

7.8.6.1 The laboratory shall document the decision rule employed when making statements of conformity in the report.
Examples
  • Documented decision rule (e.g. simple acceptance, guard band) used for conformity statements.
  • Records of customer agreement to the decision rule before reporting.
  • Decision rule stated in the report alongside the conformity statement.
7.8.6.2 The statement of conformity shall clearly identify: a) to which results it applies; b) which specifications/standards are met or not met; c) the decision rule applied.
Examples
  • Reports clearly identifying which results the conformity statement applies to.
  • Specifications or standards met or not met clearly stated.
  • Decision rule applied referenced in the conformity statement.
7.8.7 Opinions and interpretations shall be made and released by authorized personnel, with the basis documented. They shall be clearly identifiable in reports, and records of communications with the customer shall be kept.
Examples
  • Authorised personnel list for opinions and interpretations.
  • Documented basis for each opinion or interpretation in the report.
  • Records of communications with the customer about opinions and interpretations.
Clause 7.8.8 · Amendments to Reports

Amendments to reports

7.8.8.1 When the laboratory makes changes to a report, this shall be clearly identified in the report, with the reasons clearly identified where appropriate.
Examples
  • Amended reports with changes clearly marked and reasons stated.
  • Records of the original report and the amended version kept together.
  • Customer notification records when a report is amended after issue.
7.8.8.2 When changes are made after issue, the laboratory shall issue a further document including the statement "Amendment to report, serial number" or equivalent.
Examples
  • Further documents issued with "Amendment to report" statement and serial number.
  • Records showing the further document references the original report.
  • Customer acknowledgement records for the amended report.
7.8.8.3 When issuing a completely new report, the laboratory shall issue a unique identification and include a reference to the original report.
Examples
  • New reports with unique IDs referencing the original report.
  • Records showing the reason for issuing a completely new report.
  • Customer notification records for the new report and its reference to the original.
Clause 7.9 · Complaints

Complaints — process & investigation

7.9.1 / 7.9.3 The laboratory shall have a documented process to receive, evaluate and make decisions on complaints — including receiving, validating, investigating, tracking, recording and ensuring appropriate action.
Examples
  • Documented complaint procedure with responsibilities and authorities defined.
  • Complaint log tracking receipt, investigation and resolution.
  • Records showing appropriate action was taken and its effectiveness reviewed.
7.9.2 The handling process for complaints shall be available to any interested party upon request.
Examples
  • Published complaint procedure available to customers on request.
  • Records of requests and responses for the complaint procedure.
  • Customer-facing documentation describing how to make a complaint.
7.9.4 When receiving a complaint, the laboratory shall gather and verify all information to validate the complaint, and take action for complaints it is responsible for.
Examples
  • Investigation records with evidence gathered to validate the complaint.
  • Records of actions taken for complaints the laboratory is responsible for.
  • Communication records with the complainant during the investigation.
Clause 7.9 · Complaints (continued)

Complaint outcomes & closure

7.9.5 Where possible, the laboratory shall acknowledge receipt of the complaint and provide progress reports and the outcome to the complainant.
Examples
  • Acknowledgement letters or emails sent to the complainant.
  • Progress reports provided during the investigation.
  • Final outcome communication records with the complainant.
7.9.6 The individual(s) who reviews, approves or communicates the outcomes of the complaint investigation shall not be involved in the original laboratory activities.
Examples
  • Records showing the reviewer was not involved in the original activity.
  • Independence declaration for complaint reviewers.
  • Organisation chart showing separate reporting lines for complaint handling.
7.9.7 Where possible, the laboratory shall give formal notice of the end of the complaint handling to the complainant.
Examples
  • Closure letters or emails to the complainant with the outcome.
  • Records of the date and outcome of complaint handling.
  • Customer satisfaction follow-up records after complaint closure.
Clause 7.10 · Nonconforming Work

Nonconforming work

7.10.1 The laboratory shall have a procedure for dealing with nonconforming work — defining responsibilities and authorities, taking actions based on significance, notifying customers and recalling results where necessary, evaluating significance, and retaining records of nonconforming work and actions taken.
Examples
  • Documented procedure with responsibilities and authorities for managing nonconforming work.
  • Records of halted work and withheld results based on the significance of the nonconformity.
  • Customer notification and recall records where results were affected.
  • Records of cause analysis and corrective action taken.
Clause 7.11 · Control of Data & Information Management

Control of data — access, validation & checks

7.11.1 The laboratory shall have access to the data and information needed to perform laboratory activities.
Examples
  • Records showing personnel can access the data needed for their work.
  • System access logs and permissions controlling who can see what.
  • Backup and recovery arrangements ensuring data availability.
7.11.2 Information systems shall be validated, protected, maintained and controlled to ensure the integrity of data.
Examples
  • Validation records for LIMS and software before use.
  • Security controls — passwords, access levels, audit trails.
  • Maintenance and update records for information systems.
7.11.3 Calculations and data transfers shall be checked systematically. The system shall be able to record system failures and the appropriate immediate and corrective actions.
Examples
  • Records of calculation checks performed independently.
  • System failure records with corrective actions taken.
  • Audit trails showing data transfers and verification checks.
Clause 7.11 · Control of Data (continued)

Offsite systems, manuals & checks

7.11.4 The laboratory shall ensure that external providers or the operator of the LIMS complies with Section 7.11 if it is managed offsite.
Examples
  • Contracts with LIMS providers requiring compliance with Section 7.11.
  • Records of offsite management controls — access, backup, security.
  • Audit records of external provider compliance with data control requirements.
7.11.5 Instructions, manuals and reference data relevant to the LIMS shall be made readily available to personnel.
Examples
  • LIMS user manuals available to personnel at the point of use.
  • Training records on LIMS use for all relevant staff.
  • Reference data accessible through the system or controlled documents.
7.11.6 Calculations and data transfers shall be checked in an appropriate and systematic manner.
Examples
  • Independent checks of calculations before reporting.
  • Records of data transfer verification between systems.
  • Error logs and corrective actions when checks identify issues.
08

Management System Requirements

8.1 – 8.9  ·  Options A & B

Clause 8.1 · Options

Management system options

8.1.2 Option A — The management system shall address: management system documentation (8.2), control of documents (8.3), control of records (8.4), actions to address risks and opportunities (8.5), improvement (8.6), corrective action (8.7), internal audits (8.8) and management reviews (8.9).
Examples
  • Quality manual addressing Clauses 8.2 to 8.9 directly.
  • Procedures and records for each clause in the management system.
  • Management system documentation reviewed and updated annually.
8.1.3 Option B — The laboratory has established and maintained a management system in accordance with ISO 9001:2015 that supports and demonstrates consistent fulfilment of Sections 4 to 7, and fulfils at least the intent of 8.2 to 8.9.
Examples
  • ISO 9001:2015 certificate for the organisation.
  • Cross-reference matrix linking ISO 9001 clauses to MS ISO/IEC 17025 requirements.
  • Evidence that the management system supports the technical requirements of Clauses 4 to 7.
Clause 8.2 · Management System Documentation

Policies, objectives & management commitment

8.2.1 The laboratory shall establish, document and maintain policies and objectives for the fulfilment of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017 and ensure they are acknowledged and implemented at all levels.
Examples
  • Quality policy signed by top management and communicated to all staff.
  • Quality objectives with measurable targets and review dates.
  • Communication records showing policies are understood and implemented.
8.2.2 The policies and objectives shall address the competence, impartiality and consistent operation of the laboratory.
Examples
  • Objectives addressing competence, impartiality and consistency with measurable indicators.
  • Records showing these are monitored through KPIs and management review.
  • Management review records discussing competence, impartiality and consistent operation.
8.2.3 Management shall provide evidence of commitment to the development and implementation of the management system and continually improving its effectiveness.
Examples
  • Management review records showing commitment to the management system.
  • Resource allocation records for the management system.
  • Communication records from management to staff on the importance of meeting requirements.
Clause 8.2 · Management System Documentation (continued)

Documentation links & access

8.2.4 All documentation, processes, systems and records shall be included, referenced or linked to the management system.
Examples
  • Document register linking all documents to the management system.
  • Cross-reference matrix showing where each requirement is addressed.
  • Records showing all processes are covered by the management system.
8.2.5 All personnel involved in laboratory activities shall have access to the parts of the management system documentation applicable to their responsibilities.
Examples
  • Access records showing personnel can access relevant documentation.
  • Controlled distribution lists for documents by role.
  • Training records on document use and access procedures.
Clause 8.3 · Control of Management System Documents

Control of documents

8.3.1 / 8.3.2 The laboratory shall have procedures to control documents. It shall ensure: a) approval before issue; b) periodic review and update; c) changes and revision status identified; d) relevant versions available at points of use; e) unique identification; f) prevention of unintended use of obsolete documents.
Examples
  • a) Document control procedure with approval records before issue.
  • b) Periodic review records showing documents are updated as necessary.
  • c) Revision status and change history recorded for each document.
  • d) Controlled distribution ensuring relevant versions are available at points of use.
  • e) Unique document identification numbers and titles.
  • f) Obsolete documents withdrawn and archived to prevent unintended use.
Clause 8.4 · Control of Records

Control of records

8.4.1 The laboratory shall establish and retain legible records to demonstrate fulfilment of Section 8.4.
Examples
  • Legible records maintained for all management system activities.
  • Records retention schedule specifying retention times for different record types.
  • Records stored securely and accessible when needed.
8.4.2 The laboratory shall implement controls for the identification, storage, protection, back-up, archive, retrieval, retention time and disposal of records, with access readily available and consistent with confidentiality arrangements.
Examples
  • Records retention schedule with retention times and disposal methods.
  • Storage, protection and backup procedures for paper and electronic records.
  • Access controls consistent with confidentiality — restricted access, password protection.
  • Disposal records showing secure destruction of records at end of retention.
Clause 8.5 · Actions to Address Risks & Opportunities

Risks and opportunities

8.5.1 / 8.5.2 The laboratory shall consider the risks and opportunities associated with laboratory activities: a) plan actions to address them; b) integrate and implement the actions; c) evaluate the effectiveness of these actions.
Examples
  • a) Risk register identifying risks and opportunities with action plans.
  • b) Records showing actions integrated into the management system.
  • c) Effectiveness evaluation records for the actions taken.
8.5.3 Actions taken to address risks and opportunities shall be proportional to the potential impact on the validity of laboratory results.
Examples
  • Risk assessment records showing proportionality — higher risks get more attention.
  • Justification for the level of action taken based on potential impact.
  • Management review records confirming adequacy of the actions taken.
Clause 8.6 · Improvement

Improvement

8.6.1 The laboratory shall identify and select opportunities for improvement and implement any necessary actions.
Examples
  • Improvement register with identified opportunities and actions taken.
  • Records of actions implemented with responsible persons and dates.
  • Management review records discussing improvements and their effectiveness.
8.6.2 The laboratory shall seek feedback, both positive and negative, from its customers, and analyse the feedback for improving the management system, laboratory activities and customer service.
Examples
  • Customer feedback survey records with positive and negative feedback.
  • Analysis records showing how feedback was used for improvement.
  • Improvement actions resulting from feedback with effectiveness reviewed.
Clause 8.7 · Corrective Action

Corrective action

8.7.1 In the event of a nonconformity, the laboratory shall: a) react to it — control, correct and address consequences; b) evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause(s) — review, determine causes, determine if similar nonconformities exist; c) implement any action needed; d) review the effectiveness of corrective action; e) update risks and opportunities; f) make changes to the management system if necessary.
Examples
  • a) Nonconformity report with immediate control and correction actions recorded.
  • b) Root cause analysis records and determination of whether similar nonconformities exist.
  • c) Records of action implementation with dates and responsible persons.
  • d) Effectiveness review records showing the nonconformity has not recurred.
  • e) Updated risk register following the corrective action.
  • f) Management system changes documented and communicated.
8.7.2 / 8.7.3 Corrective actions shall be appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered. Records of nonconformities, causes and subsequent actions shall be retained.
Examples
  • Records showing corrective action matched the severity of the nonconformity.
  • Nonconformity, cause and action records retained for traceability.
  • Effectiveness review records confirming corrective action was appropriate.
Clause 8.8 · Internal Audits

Internal audits

8.8.1 The laboratory shall conduct internal audits to determine if the management system: a) conforms to the laboratory's own requirements including laboratory activities; b) conforms to MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017; and is effectively implemented and maintained.
Examples
  • Internal audit programme covering all clauses over the accreditation cycle.
  • Audit reports with findings and conclusions against the standard and internal requirements.
  • Records showing the management system is effectively implemented and maintained.
8.8.2 The laboratory shall: a) plan, establish, implement and maintain an audit programme; b) define audit criteria and scope; c) report results to relevant management; d) implement correction and corrective actions without undue delay; e) retain records as evidence.
Examples
  • a) Documented audit programme with frequency, methods and responsibilities.
  • b) Audit criteria and scope defined for each audit.
  • c) Audit results reported to relevant management.
  • d) Corrective action records from audits implemented without undue delay.
  • e) Audit records retained as evidence of the programme and results.
Clause 8.9 · Management Reviews

Management reviews

8.9.1 The laboratory management shall review its management system to ensure continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness, including the stated policies and objectives related to the fulfilment of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017.
Examples
  • Management review meeting minutes showing review of suitability, adequacy and effectiveness.
  • Records showing policies and objectives were reviewed against the standard.
  • Attendance records showing top management participates in the review.
8.9.2 The laboratory shall record inputs to management review including: changes in internal/external issues, fulfilment of objectives, suitability of policies/procedures, status of previous actions, internal audit outcomes, corrective actions, external assessments, PT/ILC results, customer and personnel feedback, complaints, improvement effectiveness, resource adequacy, risk identification results, validity assurance outcomes, and other relevant factors.
Examples
  • Records of all required inputs — changes, objectives, policies, previous actions, audit outcomes, corrective actions.
  • External assessment, PT/ILC results, customer and personnel feedback documented as inputs.
  • Complaints, improvements, resources, risks, validity assurance and other factors included in the review.
8.9.3 The outputs of management review shall be recorded, including decisions and actions related to improvement, resource needs and changes to the management system.
Examples
  • Management review outputs with decisions and actions recorded.
  • Records of improvement, resource needs and system changes from the review.
  • Follow-up records showing actions completed with responsible persons and dates.
Summary

Key takeaways

  • ▸ Clause 4 — Impartiality and confidentiality are ongoing obligations.
  • ▸ Clause 5 — The laboratory must be a legal entity with a defined, documented scope and clear responsibilities.
  • ▸ Clause 6 — Competent people, suitable facilities, calibrated equipment, unbroken traceability and controlled external providers.
  • ▸ Clause 7 — The technical process: valid methods, controlled records, sound uncertainty, valid results and defensible reports.
  • ▸ Clause 8 — A living management system: risks, improvement, corrective action, internal audits and management review.
  • ▸ Annexes — Metrological traceability and management system options.
