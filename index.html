04

General Requirements

4.1 Impartiality  ·  4.2 Confidentiality

Clause 4.1 · Impartiality

Impartiality

ClauseRequirement
4.1.1Laboratory activities shall be undertaken impartially.
4.1.2Top management shall demonstrate commitment to impartiality.
4.1.3The laboratory shall be responsible for the impartiality of its laboratory activities and shall not allow commercial, financial or other pressures to compromise impartiality.
4.1.4The laboratory shall identify risks to its impartiality on an ongoing basis. This shall include risks arising from its activities, its relationships, and the relationships of its personnel.
4.1.5The laboratory shall eliminate or minimize the risks to impartiality identified.

Examples in practice

  • Signed impartiality declaration forms for all employees and contracted personnel, refreshed annually.
  • A risk register identifying threats from commercial relationships — for example, a parent company that also supplies products to the laboratory's customers.
  • Management review minutes recording the annual review of impartiality risks and the mitigation actions taken.
Clause 4.2 · Confidentiality

Confidentiality

ClauseRequirement
4.2.1The laboratory shall be responsible for the confidentiality of information obtained or created during the performance of laboratory activities, and for the proprietary rights of its customers.
4.2.2The laboratory shall notify its customer when it is legally obliged to release confidential information.
4.2.3Information about the customer received from sources other than the customer shall be kept confidential; the source shall not be revealed to the customer unless agreed by the source.
4.2.4Personnel shall keep confidential all information obtained or created during the performance of laboratory activities.

Examples in practice

  • Signed confidentiality agreements for employees, contractors and temporary staff, with defined consequences for breach.
  • Contract clauses stating that the laboratory will notify the customer before legally compelled to disclose confidential information.
  • Separate storage and access controls for customer-supplied information received from third parties, so the source is not revealed to the customer without consent.

Structural Requirements

5.1 – 5.7  ·  Legal entity, management, scope, organisation & responsibility

Clause 5.1 – 5.3

Legal entity, management & scope of activities

ClauseRequirement
5.1The laboratory or the organisation of which it is part shall be a legal entity legally responsible for its laboratory activities.
5.2The laboratory shall identify management that has overall responsibility for the laboratory.
5.3The laboratory shall define and document the range of laboratory activities for which it conforms to MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017. This shall cover work carried out in permanent facilities, sites away from permanent facilities, associated temporary or mobile facilities, and customer facilities. The activities shall conform to customer and regulatory requirements.

Examples in practice

  • ACRA business registration certificate and company registration number kept on file as evidence of legal entity status.
  • Organisation chart showing the laboratory's position within the parent organisation and the reporting line to top management.
  • Documented scope of accreditation listing permanent laboratory tests, mobile facility activities and customer-site work.
Clause 5.4 – 5.7

Organisation, responsibility & personnel

ClauseRequirement
5.4Laboratory activities shall be performed in a way that meets the requirements of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017, the laboratory's customers, regulatory authorities and the organisations providing recognition.
5.5 The laboratory shall:
a) define the organisation and management structure of the laboratory;
b) specify the responsibility, authority and interrelationship of all personnel who manage, perform or verify work affecting results;
c) document necessary procedures to ensure consistent application of its activities and the validity of its results.
5.6 The laboratory shall have authorized and sufficient personnel to:
a) implement, maintain and improve the management system;
b) identify deviations and initiate actions to minimize or prevent such deviations;
c) report to management on the performance of the management system and any need for improvements;
d) ensure the effectiveness of laboratory activities.
5.7The laboratory shall communicate to staff about the effectiveness and integrity of the management system when changes are made to it.

Examples in practice

  • Job descriptions for the laboratory manager, technical manager, quality manager and approved signatories, defining responsibilities and authority.
  • Procedure for communicating management system changes to all staff — for example, briefing sessions, email notices and training records.
  • Staffing plan showing sufficient authorised personnel for the current and forecast workload, with named deputies.

Resource Requirements

6.1 General  ·  6.2 Personnel  ·  6.3 Facilities  ·  6.4 Equipment  ·  6.5 Traceability  ·  6.6 External providers

Clause 6.1 – 6.2 · Personnel

Resources & personnel competence

6.1 General

The laboratory shall have sufficient personnel, facilities, equipment, systems and support services necessary to manage and perform its laboratory activities.

ClauseRequirement
6.2.1All personnel acting in the laboratory shall be impartial, competent and work in accordance with the management system.
6.2.2 The laboratory shall document the competence requirements for each function that may influence the results of laboratory activities, including: education, qualification, training required, technical knowledge, skills and experience.
6.2.3Personnel shall have the competence to perform the laboratory activities for which they are responsible.
6.2.4The duties, responsibilities and authorities of laboratory personnel shall be communicated to them.
6.2.5 The laboratory shall have procedures and retain records for:
a) determining the competence requirements;
b) to f) selection, training, supervision, authorization, and monitoring of competence of personnel performing laboratory activities.
6.2.6Personnel shall be authorized to perform specific laboratory activities.

Examples in practice

  • Training and competency matrix linking each function to the required education, training, technical knowledge, skills and experience.
  • Signed authorisation records naming approved signatories for specific test or calibration methods.
  • Personnel files containing CVs, qualification certificates, training records and competency assessment results.
Clause 6.3 · Facilities & Environmental Conditions

Facilities and environmental conditions

ClauseRequirement
6.3.1The laboratory shall document and ensure control of the facilities and environmental conditions necessary for the performance of laboratory activities.
6.3.2Facilities and environmental conditions shall be suitable and shall not adversely affect the validity of results.
6.3.3The laboratory shall monitor, control and record the environmental conditions.
6.3.4 The laboratory shall monitor and periodically review the measures to control facilities, including:
  • Access to and use of laboratory areas
  • Prevention of contamination, interference or other adverse influences
  • Effective separation between areas with incompatible laboratory activities
6.3.5The laboratory shall ensure compliance to Section 6.3 when laboratory activities are performed at sites or facilities outside its permanent control.

Examples in practice

  • Temperature and humidity monitoring records for a controlled weighing room or calibration laboratory.
  • Access control system restricting entry to authorised personnel, with visitor logs and escort arrangements.
  • Procedure for performing tests at customer sites, including environmental checks, temporary facility setup and segregation from incompatible activities.
Clause 6.4 · Equipment

Equipment — availability, handling & calibration

ClauseRequirement
6.4.1The laboratory shall have access to equipment required for the correct performance of laboratory activities, and which can influence results.
6.4.2The laboratory shall ensure compliance to Section 6.4 when using equipment outside its permanent control.
6.4.3The laboratory shall have a procedure for handling, transport, storage, use and planned maintenance of equipment.
6.4.4The laboratory shall verify that equipment conforms to specified requirements before being placed or returned into service.
6.4.5 – 6.4.6Measuring equipment shall be calibrated and capable of achieving the measurement accuracy or measurement uncertainty required to provide a valid result.
6.4.7The laboratory shall have an established calibration programme and shall review the suitability and frequency of calibration.
6.4.8Equipment requiring calibration or which has a defined period of validity shall be labelled, coded or identified.

Examples in practice

  • Calibration programme with due dates, intervals and acceptance criteria for all measuring equipment.
  • Equipment master list with unique IDs, locations, calibration intervals and responsible personnel.
  • Verification records showing a newly purchased instrument was checked against specified requirements before being placed into service.
Clause 6.4 · Equipment (continued)

Equipment — malfunction, checks & records

ClauseRequirement
6.4.9The laboratory shall have a process for handling equipment that no longer meets specified requirements, including examining the effect of the deviation from specified requirements.
6.4.11The laboratory shall perform periodic checks or updates to maintain confidence in the performance of its equipment, including reference values or correction factors.
6.4.12The laboratory shall take practicable measures to prevent unintended adjustments of equipment from invalidating results.
6.4.13 The laboratory shall retain records for equipment which can influence laboratory activities, including:
  • a) Identity of equipment, including software and firmware version
  • b) Manufacturer's name, type identification, serial number or other unique identification
  • c) Evidence of verification that equipment conforms with specified requirements
  • d) Current location
  • e) Calibration dates, results, adjustments, acceptance criteria, due date / interval
  • f) Documentation of reference materials, results, acceptance criteria, relevant dates, period of validity
  • g) Maintenance plan and maintenance carried out to date
  • h) Details of any damage, malfunction, modification to, or repair of, the equipment

Examples in practice

  • Out-of-service tags and an impact assessment record when an instrument fails calibration, showing which results may be affected.
  • Interim checks — for example, daily balance checks or weekly thermometer verification — recorded between annual calibrations.
  • Equipment history file containing damage, repair and modification records, with dates and responsible personnel.
Clause 6.5 · Metrological Traceability

Metrological traceability

ClauseRequirement
6.5.1The laboratory shall establish and maintain metrological traceability of its measurement results by means of a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the measurement uncertainty, linking them to an appropriate reference.
6.5.2Measurement results shall be traceable to the International System of Units (SI) where possible.
6.5.3Where SI traceability is not possible, traceability shall be to an appropriate reference, such as calibration provided by a competent laboratory, certified reference materials by a competent producer, or direct realization of SI units.

Examples in practice

  • Calibration certificates from a national metrology institute (NMI) or an accredited calibration laboratory, showing traceability to SI units.
  • Certified reference materials (CRMs) with certificates stating traceability to SI units and the associated uncertainty.
  • Traceability chart showing the unbroken chain from working standards through reference standards to the SI realisation.
Clause 6.6 · Externally Provided Products & Services

Externally provided products and services

ClauseRequirement
6.6.1The laboratory shall ensure the suitability of externally provided products and services that affect laboratory activities.
6.6.2 The laboratory shall have procedures and records for:
  • a) defining, reviewing and approving the laboratory's requirements for externally provided products and services
  • b) criteria for evaluation, selection, monitoring of performance and re-evaluation of external providers
6.6.3The laboratory shall communicate its requirements to external providers for the products and services to be provided.

Examples in practice

  • Approved supplier list with evaluation criteria, performance monitoring notes and re-evaluation dates.
  • Purchase specifications defining the technical requirements for calibration services, reference materials or subcontracted tests.
  • Contracts with external calibration providers stating the required traceability, uncertainty and reporting requirements.

Process Requirements

7.1 – 7.11  ·  The technical heart of the standard

Clause 7.1 · Review of Requests, Tenders & Contracts

Review of requests, tenders and contracts

  • 7.1.1 — The laboratory shall have a procedure for the review of requests, tenders and contracts. The review shall ensure that:
    • Requirements are adequately defined, documented and understood
    • The laboratory has the capability and resources to meet them
    • The appropriate method is selected and capable of meeting the customer's requirements
  • 7.1.2 — The laboratory shall:
    • Inform the customer of any inappropriate or outdated methods, and ensure the contract is acceptable to both the laboratory and the customer
    • Ensure that deviations requested by the customer do not impact the integrity of the laboratory or the validity of the results
    • Ensure the decision rule is clearly defined, communicated and agreed by both the laboratory and the customer when a statement of conformity is required
    • Notify customers of any deviations from the contract and, if these occur after work has commenced, repeat the contract review and notify all affected personnel
  • 7.1.3 — The laboratory shall cooperate with customers or their representatives in clarifying the customer's request and in monitoring the laboratory's performance in relation to the work performed.
  • 7.1.4 — Methods shall be selected and verified or validated in accordance with Clause 7.2.

Examples in practice

  • Signed contract review records for each customer request, showing capability, resources and method selection were checked.
  • Documented decision rule agreed with the customer when a statement of conformity is required — for example, simple acceptance or a guard band.
  • Records of customer notification when a deviation from the contract occurs after work has commenced, including the repeated review and affected personnel notified.
Clause 7.2.1 · Selection and Verification of Methods

Selection and verification of methods

ClauseRequirement
7.2.1.1All methods, procedures and supporting documentation shall be appropriate to laboratory activities, relevant, up to date, and readily available to personnel.
7.2.1.2The laboratory shall ensure that it uses the most appropriate version of a method.
7.2.1.3The laboratory shall select and inform the customer of the method chosen if the customer does not specify the method.
7.2.1.4The laboratory shall verify that it can properly perform the methods and achieve the required performance before introducing them.
7.2.1.5Records of verification shall be retained.
7.2.1.6The laboratory shall have a formal plan for method development, performed by competent personnel with adequate resources. Periodic reviews shall ensure that the needs of the customer are fulfilled.
7.2.1.7Any deviations to methods shall be documented, justified, authorized and accepted by the customer prior to performing them.

Examples in practice

  • Verification records showing a new test method was successfully performed and achieved the required performance before use.
  • Controlled method library with current versions available at the bench, and obsolete versions clearly withdrawn.
  • Documented customer acceptance for a deviation from a standard method, signed before the work was performed.
Clause 7.2.2 · Validation of Methods

Validation of methods

ClauseRequirement
7.2.2.1 The laboratory shall validate:
  • Non-standard methods
  • Laboratory-developed methods
  • Standard methods used outside their intended scope
  • Any validated methods that have since been modified
7.2.2.2 Validation records shall include:
  • The validation procedure used
  • Specification of the requirements
  • Determination of the performance characteristics of the method
  • Results obtained
  • A statement on the validity of the method, detailing its fitness for the intended use
7.2.2.3The performance characteristics of validated methods shall be relevant to the customer's needs and consistent with specified requirements.

Examples in practice

  • Validation report for a laboratory-developed method, including selectivity, linearity, accuracy, precision and uncertainty.
  • Records of method modification and re-validation when a standard method has been adapted or changed.
  • Statement of fitness for intended use for a non-standard sampling or test method, signed by the technical manager.
Clause 7.3 – 7.4

Sampling & handling of test or calibration items

7.3 Sampling

  • 7.3.1 — The laboratory shall have a sampling plan and method for performing sampling activities.
    • The sampling plan and method shall be available at the site where sampling is performed.
    • The sampling method shall address the factors to be controlled to ensure the validity of subsequent testing or calibration results.
  • 7.3.2 — Sampling data shall be recorded as part of the laboratory activity.
  • 7.3.3 — Deviations from the sampling plan or method shall be recorded and reported.

7.4 Handling of test or calibration items

ClauseRequirement
7.4.1The laboratory shall have a system for unambiguous identification of test or calibration items that ensures no physical confusion or when referred to in records or documents. The identification shall be retained whilst under the responsibility of the laboratory.
7.4.2The laboratory shall take precautions to avoid deterioration, contamination, loss or damage to the item during handling, transport, storage, preparation and retention.
7.4.3Upon receipt of items which have deviated from specified conditions, the laboratory shall record these deviations and notify the customer for instructions. The results of this consultation shall be recorded.
7.4.4For items requiring storage under specific environmental conditions, the laboratory shall maintain, monitor and record these conditions.

Examples in practice

  • Sampling plan available at the sampling site, describing the sampling method and the factors to be controlled.
  • Unique item identification labels that prevent mix-ups between samples, with the identification retained throughout the laboratory's custody.
  • Records of item receipt conditions, including any deviations from specified conditions notified to the customer with their instructions.
Clause 7.5 – 7.6

Technical records & evaluation of measurement uncertainty

7.5 Technical records

ClauseRequirement
7.5.1 The laboratory shall retain and maintain technical records containing sufficient information to enable repetition of the laboratory activity under conditions as close as possible to the original. Records shall include:
  • Date and identity of personnel responsible for the laboratory activity and checking of data and results
  • Factors affecting measurement results and its associated measurement uncertainty
  • Evaluation of measurement uncertainty
7.5.2 Amendments to technical reports shall be trackable to previous versions or original observations. The original and amended data and files shall be kept. Records shall include:
  • Date of alteration
  • Indication of altered aspects
  • Personnel responsible for the alterations

7.6 Evaluation of measurement uncertainty

  • 7.6.1 — The laboratory shall identify the contributions to measurement uncertainty using appropriate methods of analysis, including those arising from sampling.
  • 7.6.2 — For laboratories performing calibrations, including of its own equipment, the measurement uncertainty for all calibrations shall be evaluated.
  • 7.6.3 — For laboratories performing testing, the measurement uncertainty shall be evaluated. If it is not possible to evaluate the measurement uncertainty, an estimation of the uncertainty shall be made.

Examples in practice

  • Technical records with date, personnel identity and raw data, sufficient to repeat the activity under conditions as close as possible to the original.
  • Uncertainty budget for a calibration method, identifying each contribution and its magnitude.
  • Amendment log showing the date of alteration, the altered aspects and the personnel responsible for the change.
Clause 7.7 · Ensuring the Validity of Results

Ensuring the validity of results

ClauseRequirement
7.7.1The laboratory shall have a procedure for monitoring the validity of results. The monitoring shall be planned and reviewed periodically.
7.7.2The laboratory shall compare its results with other laboratories, for example through participation in proficiency testing or inter-laboratory comparisons.
7.7.3The laboratory shall analyse the data or results of the monitoring activities and take appropriate actions when these results fall outside pre-defined criteria.

Examples in practice

  • Participation in proficiency testing schemes with satisfactory results, and records of investigation for any unsatisfactory result.
  • Control charts used to monitor results over time, with pre-defined criteria for triggering action.
  • Procedure for analysing monitoring data and initiating corrective action when results fall outside pre-defined criteria.
Clause 7.8.1 – 7.8.2 · Reporting of Results

Reporting of results — general & common requirements

  • 7.8.1.1 — The laboratory shall review and authorize the results prior to release. Any results not reported to the customer shall be made readily available.

7.8.2.1 — The report shall contain at least the following information:

  • a) A title (e.g. "Test Report", "Calibration Certificate" or "Report of Sampling")
  • b) Name and address of the laboratory
  • c) Location of performance of the laboratory activities
  • d) Unique identification that all components are recognized as a portion of a complete report, and a clear identification of the end
  • e) Name and contact information of the customer
  • f) Identification of the method used
  • g) A description, unambiguous identification, and, when necessary, the condition of the item
  • h) Date of receipt of the item(s) and the date of sampling, where critical to validity
  • i) Date(s) of performance of the laboratory activity
  • j) Date of issue of the report
  • k) Reference to the sampling plan and sampling method used, where relevant
  • l) A statement that the results relate only to the items tested, calibrated or sampled
  • m) The results with, where appropriate, the units of measurement
  • n) Additions to, deviations, or exclusions from the method
  • o) Identification of the person(s) authorizing the report
  • p) Clear identification when results are from external providers

Examples in practice

  • Report review and authorisation checklist used before any result is released to the customer.
  • Report template containing all required common elements — title, laboratory address, unique identification, method, item description, dates, results and authorisation.
  • Clear identification in the report of which information and/or sample is provided by the customer (7.8.2.2).
Clause 7.8.3 – 7.8.5 · Specific Requirements

Test, calibration & sampling reports

7.8.3 — Specific requirements for test reports

The test report shall contain the following where necessary:

  • a) Information on specific test conditions, such as environmental conditions
  • b) Where relevant, a statement of conformity with requirements or specifications
  • c) Where applicable, the measurement uncertainty presented in the same unit as the measurand, or in a term relative to the measurand (e.g. percent)
  • d) Where appropriate, opinions and interpretations
  • e) Additional information which may be required by specific methods, authorities, customers or groups of customers

7.8.4 — Specific requirements for calibration reports

  • a) The measurement uncertainty of the measurement result, presented in the same unit as the measurand or in a term relative to the measurand (e.g. percent)
  • The conditions (e.g. environmental) under which the calibrations were made that have an influence on the measurement results
  • A statement identifying how the measurements are metrologically traceable
  • The results before and after any adjustment or repair, if available
  • Where relevant, a statement of conformity with requirements or specifications
  • Where appropriate, opinions and interpretations
  • 7.8.4.3 — Calibration certificates or labels shall not contain any recommendation on the calibration interval unless agreed with the customer.

7.8.5 — Specific requirements for sampling reports

  • The date of sampling
  • Unique identification of the item or material sampled
  • The location of sampling, including any diagrams, sketches or photographs
  • A reference to the sampling plan and sampling method
  • Details of any environmental conditions during sampling that affect the interpretation of results
  • Information required to evaluate measurement uncertainty for subsequent testing or calibration

Examples in practice

  • Test report stating measurement uncertainty when it is relevant to the validity or application of the test results, or when the customer requires it.
  • Calibration certificate stating metrological traceability and the results before and after any adjustment or repair.
  • Sampling report including the date of sampling, location, sampling plan reference and environmental conditions during sampling.
Clause 7.8.6 – 7.8.8

Statements of conformity, opinions & amendments

7.8.6 — Reporting statements of conformity

  • 7.8.6.1 — The laboratory shall document the decision rule employed when making statements of conformity in the report.
  • 7.8.6.2 — When reporting on the statement of conformity, the statement shall clearly identify:
    a) to which results the statement of conformity applies;
    b) which specifications, standards or parts thereof are met or not met;
    c) the decision rule applied.

7.8.7 — Reporting opinions and interpretations

  • 7.8.7.1 — Opinions and interpretations shall be made and released by authorized personnel. The laboratory shall document the basis on which the opinions and interpretations are made.
  • 7.8.7.2 — It shall be clearly identifiable in the reports when the laboratory makes opinions and interpretations based on the results obtained from the tested or calibrated item.
  • 7.8.7.3 — The laboratory shall keep records of any communications of opinions and interpretations with the customer.

7.8.8 — Amendments to reports

  • 7.8.8.1 — When the laboratory makes any changes to a report, this shall be clearly identified in the report. Where appropriate, the reasons for the changes shall be clearly identified.
  • 7.8.8.2 — When changes are made to a report after it has been issued, the laboratory shall issue it as a further document which includes the statement "Amendment to report, serial number" or other equivalent form of wording.
  • 7.8.8.3 — When issuing a completely new report, the laboratory shall issue a unique identification to this report and include a reference to the original report.

Examples in practice

  • Documented decision rule (e.g. simple acceptance, guard band) used in conformity statements, with the rule clearly stated in the report.
  • Amended report issued as a further document with the statement "Amendment to report, serial number…" and the reasons for the change.
  • New report with a unique ID referencing the original report, when a completely new report is issued after changes.
Clause 7.9 · Complaints

Complaints

ClauseRequirement
7.9.1 / 7.9.3 The laboratory shall have a documented process to receive, evaluate and make decisions on complaints. The process for handling complaints shall contain at least the following elements:
  • a) Description of the process for receiving, validating, investigating the complaint, and deciding what actions are to be taken in response to it
  • b) Tracking and recording complaints, including actions undertaken to resolve them
  • c) Ensuring that any appropriate action is taken
7.9.2The handling process for complaints shall be available to any interested party upon request.
7.9.4When receiving the complaint, the laboratory shall gather and verify all information to validate the complaint, and take action to deal with any complaints that it is responsible for.
7.9.5Where possible, the laboratory shall acknowledge receipt of the complaint, and provide the complainant with progress reports and the outcome.
7.9.6The individual(s) who reviews, approves or communicates the outcomes of the complaint investigation to the complainant shall not be involved in the original laboratory activities.
7.9.7Where possible, the laboratory shall give formal notice of the end of the complaint handling to the complainant.

Examples in practice

  • Documented complaint procedure available to customers on request, describing how complaints are received, validated, investigated and decided.
  • Complaint log tracking receipt, investigation, actions taken and closure, with the outcome communicated to the complainant.
  • Formal notice of the end of complaint handling sent to the complainant, with the person reviewing the outcome not involved in the original laboratory activity.
Clause 7.10 – 7.11

Nonconforming work & control of data and information management

7.10 — Nonconforming work

  • 7.10.1 — The laboratory shall have a procedure for dealing with nonconforming work. The procedure shall ensure that:
    • a) The responsibilities and authorities for the management of nonconforming work are defined
    • b) Actions (including halting work, withholding results) are taken based on the significance of the nonconformity
    • c) The customer is notified and, where necessary, results are recalled
    • d) The significance of the nonconforming work is evaluated and appropriate action taken
    • e) Records are retained of nonconforming work, actions taken and the results of any corrective action

7.11 — Control of data and information management

  • 7.11.1 — The laboratory shall have access to the data and information needed to perform laboratory activities.
  • 7.11.2 — Information systems shall be validated, protected, maintained and controlled to ensure the integrity of data.
  • 7.11.3 — Calculations and data transfers shall be checked in an appropriate and systematic manner. The system shall be able to record system failures and the appropriate immediate and corrective actions.
  • 7.11.4 — External providers or the operator of the LIMS shall comply with the requirements of Section 7.11 if it is managed offsite.
  • 7.11.5 — Instructions, manuals and reference data relevant to the laboratory information management system(s) shall be made readily available to personnel.
  • 7.11.6 — Calculations and data transfers shall be checked in an appropriate and systematic manner.

Examples in practice

  • Procedure for halting work and withholding results when nonconforming work is detected, with defined responsibilities and authorities.
  • Records of nonconforming work, including cause analysis, actions taken and the results of any corrective action.
  • Validated LIMS with audit trails, backup, controlled access and documented checks of calculations and data transfers.
08

Management System Requirements

8.1 – 8.9  ·  Options A & B

Clause 8.1 · Options

Management system options

Option A (8.1.2)

The management system of a laboratory shall address the following:

  • Management system documentation (8.2)
  • Control of management system documents (8.3)
  • Control of records (8.4)
  • Actions to address risks and opportunities (8.5)
  • Improvement (8.6)
  • Corrective action (8.7)
  • Internal audits (8.8)
  • Management reviews (8.9)

Option B (8.1.3)

The laboratory has established and maintained a management system in accordance with ISO 9001:2015, and that is capable of supporting and demonstrating the consistent fulfilment of the requirements of Sections 4 to 7.

This also fulfils at least the intent of the management requirements specified in 8.2 to 8.9.

Examples in practice

  • Quality manual documenting the management system per Option A, addressing Clauses 8.2 to 8.9 directly.
  • ISO 9001:2015 certificate for the organisation choosing Option B, with a cross-reference matrix linking ISO 9001 clauses to MS ISO/IEC 17025 requirements.
  • Evidence that the chosen option supports consistent fulfilment of the technical requirements of Clauses 4 to 7.
Clause 8.2 · Management System Documentation

Management system documentation

  • 8.2.1 — The laboratory shall establish, document and maintain policies and objectives for the fulfilment of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017 and ensure that these policies are acknowledged and implemented at all levels of the laboratory organisation.
  • 8.2.2 — The policies and objectives shall address the competence, impartiality and consistent operation of the laboratory.
  • 8.2.3 — Management shall provide evidence of commitment to the development and implementation of the management system and continually improving its effectiveness.
  • 8.2.4 — All documentation, processes, systems, records, etc. shall be included, referenced or linked to the management system.
  • 8.2.5 — All personnel involved in laboratory activities shall have access to the parts of the management system documentation and related information that are applicable to their responsibilities.

Examples in practice

  • Quality policy signed by top management and communicated to all staff, with acknowledgement records.
  • Quality objectives addressing competence, impartiality and consistent operation, with measurable targets and review dates.
  • Management review records demonstrating commitment to the development, implementation and continual improvement of the management system.
Clause 8.3 – 8.4

Control of management system documents & records

8.3 — Control of management system documents

The laboratory shall have procedures to control the documents (internal and external). The laboratory shall ensure that:

  • a) Documents are approved for adequacy prior to issue by authorized personnel
  • b) Documents are periodically reviewed, and updated as necessary
  • c) Changes and the current revision status of documents are identified
  • d) Relevant versions of applicable documents are available at points of use and, where necessary, their distribution is controlled
  • e) Documents are uniquely identified
  • f) The unintended use of obsolete documents is prevented

8.4 — Control of records

  • 8.4.1 — The laboratory shall establish and retain legible records to demonstrate fulfilment of Section 8.4.
  • 8.4.2 — The laboratory shall implement the controls needed for the identification, storage, protection, back-up, archive, retrieval, retention time and disposal of its records. Access to these records shall be readily available and consistent with the confidentiality arrangements.

Examples in practice

  • Document control procedure with a master list, revision status, approval records and controlled distribution.
  • Records retention schedule specifying retention times, storage conditions and disposal methods for different record types.
  • Controlled access to records consistent with confidentiality arrangements, with back-up and archive arrangements in place.
Clause 8.5 – 8.6

Actions to address risks and opportunities & improvement

8.5 — Actions to address risks and opportunities

  • 8.5.1 / 8.5.2 — The laboratory shall consider the risks and opportunities associated with laboratory activities. It shall:
    • a) plan actions to address these risks and opportunities;
    • b) integrate and implement the actions into its management system;
    • c) evaluate the effectiveness of these actions.
  • 8.5.3 — The actions taken to address risks and opportunities shall be proportional to the potential impact on the validity of laboratory results.

8.6 — Improvement

  • 8.6.1 — The laboratory shall identify and select opportunities for improvement and implement any necessary actions.
  • 8.6.2 — The laboratory shall seek feedback, both positive and negative, from its customers. The laboratory shall analyse the feedback for purposes of improving the management system, laboratory activities and customer service.

Examples in practice

  • Risk register identifying risks and opportunities associated with laboratory activities, with action plans and responsible persons.
  • Customer feedback survey analysed for improvement opportunities, with actions taken and effectiveness reviewed.
  • Effectiveness review of actions taken to address risks and opportunities, with updates to the risk register as needed.
Clause 8.7 · Corrective Action

Corrective action

8.7.1 — In the event of a nonconformity, the laboratory shall:

  • a) React to the nonconformity and, as applicable:
    • take action to control and correct it;
    • address the consequences.
  • b) Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause(s) of the nonconformity, to prevent recurrence or occurrence elsewhere, by:
    • reviewing and analysing the nonconformity;
    • determining the causes of the nonconformity;
    • determining if similar nonconformities exist, or could potentially occur.
  • c) Implement any action needed.
  • d) Review the effectiveness of any corrective action taken.
  • e) Update risks and opportunities determined during planning, if necessary.
  • f) Make changes to the management system, if necessary.
  • 8.7.2 / 8.7.3 — Corrective actions shall be appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered. Records of nonconformities, causes and any subsequent actions taken shall be retained.

Examples in practice

  • Nonconformity report with root cause analysis and corrective action, signed by the responsible manager.
  • Effectiveness review of the corrective action taken, with evidence that the nonconformity has not recurred.
  • Updated risk register following the corrective action, reflecting any changes to risks and opportunities.
Clause 8.8 · Internal Audits

Internal audits

  • 8.8.1 — The laboratory shall conduct internal audits to determine if the management system:
    • a) conforms to the laboratory's own requirements for its management system, including the laboratory activities;
    • b) conforms to the requirements of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017;
    • is effectively implemented and maintained.
  • 8.8.2 — The laboratory shall:
    • a) plan, establish, implement and maintain an audit programme including the frequency, methods, responsibilities, planning requirements and reporting;
    • b) define the audit criteria and scope for each audit;
    • c) ensure that the results of the audits are reported to relevant management;
    • d) implement appropriate correction and corrective actions without undue delay;
    • e) retain records as evidence of the implementation of the audit programme and the audit results.

Examples in practice

  • Internal audit programme covering all clauses over the accreditation cycle, with frequency, methods and responsibilities defined.
  • Internal audit report with findings, audit criteria and scope, reported to relevant management.
  • Records showing corrective actions from audits implemented without undue delay, with effectiveness reviewed.
Clause 8.9 · Management Reviews

Management reviews

  • 8.9.1 — The laboratory management shall review its management system to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness, including the stated policies and objectives related to the fulfilment of MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017.
  • 8.9.2 — The laboratory shall record the following inputs to management review:
    • a) Changes in internal and external issues that are relevant to the laboratory
    • b) Fulfilment of objectives
    • c) Suitability of policies and procedures
    • d) Status of actions from previous management reviews
    • e) Outcome of recent internal audits
    • f) Corrective actions
    • g) Assessments by external bodies
    • h) Results of proficiency testing / inter-laboratory comparisons
    • i) Customer and personnel feedback
    • j) Complaints
    • k) Effectiveness of improvements implemented
    • l) Adequacy of resources
    • m) Results of risk identification
    • n) Outcomes of the assurance of the validity of results
    • o) Other relevant factors such as monitoring activities and training
  • 8.9.3 — The outputs of management review shall be recorded, including decisions and actions related to improvement, resource needs and changes to the management system.

Examples in practice

  • Management review meeting minutes with all required inputs recorded, including audit outcomes, corrective actions and customer feedback.
  • Decisions and actions from management review with responsible persons and deadlines, recorded as outputs.
  • Follow-up on actions from previous management reviews, with status updates and evidence of completion.
Annex A · Metrological Traceability

Metrological traceability

ClauseRequirement
A.1Metrological traceability shall be established and maintained for measurement results.
A.2Traceability shall be demonstrated through an unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the measurement uncertainty.
A.3Where applicable, traceability shall be to the SI units, or to an appropriate reference such as certified reference materials, consensus standards or specified methods.

Examples in practice

  • Calibration certificates from an NMI demonstrating traceability to SI units, kept on file for each reference standard.
  • Certified reference materials with traceability statements and uncertainty values, used for calibration or quality control.
  • Documented traceability chain for working standards, showing each link from the working standard to the SI realisation.
Annex B · Management System Options

Management system options

Option A

The laboratory implements a management system that addresses Clauses 8.2 to 8.9 directly as set out in MS ISO/IEC 17025:2017.

Option B

The laboratory has a management system in accordance with ISO 9001:2015 that supports and demonstrates consistent fulfilment of Clauses 4 to 7, and fulfils at least the intent of Clauses 8.2 to 8.9.

Examples in practice

  • Option A: documented management system addressing Clauses 8.2 to 8.9 directly, with procedures, records and reviews in place.
  • Option B: ISO 9001:2015 certificate plus a cross-reference matrix linking ISO 9001 clauses to MS ISO/IEC 17025 requirements.
  • Evidence that the chosen option supports consistent fulfilment of the technical requirements of Clauses 4 to 7, including competence, methods, equipment and reporting.
Summary

Key takeaways

  • Clause 4 — Impartiality and confidentiality are ongoing obligations.
  • Clause 5 — The laboratory must be a legal entity with a defined, documented scope and clear responsibilities.
  • Clause 6 — Competent people, suitable facilities, calibrated equipment, unbroken traceability and controlled external providers.
  • Clause 7 — The technical process: valid methods, controlled records, sound uncertainty, valid results and defensible reports.
  • Clause 8 — A living management system: risks, improvement, corrective action, internal audits and management review.
  • Annexes — Metrological traceability and management system options.
